## The American Document: Lewis, Hemingway, Faulkner

*Part of [[The Etymological Archive]] | Chapter thread: the iceberg theory — clarity achieved through omission.*

---

These words are about what is *not* said. About what sits beneath the surface and holds everything up. Hemingway never chose a word without having discarded ten.

---

## The Words

**TERSE**
- Origin: Latin *tersus* (wiped clean, scrubbed)
- Weight: Cleaned of everything unnecessary. Not short — *stripped back*.
- Avoid instead: *short, blunt*
- Exercise: Rewrite a sentence until it is *terse*. How many words did you lose?

**LACONIC**
- Origin: Greek *lakonikos* — the Spartans of Laconia were known for answering with one word where others used ten
- Weight: Brevity as a philosophical and military posture. To say little is to possess what you know.
- Historical note: Philip of Macedon sent a message to Sparta: *"If I enter Laconia, I will level it to the ground."* Sparta replied with one word: *"If."*
- Connection: Hemingway's dialogues are laconic. That is not minimalism — it is control.

**STARK**
- Origin: Old English *stearc* (rigid, strong, unyielding)
- Weight: Hard without softness, real without apology. Anglo-Saxon at its core.
- Avoid instead: *bare, empty*
- Connection: The American document is *stark* in a way European prose does not permit itself.

**VERNACULAR**
- Origin: Latin *vernaculus* (domestic, from the language of household slaves)
- Weight: The people's language, not the educated class's. In literature: a political choice to write as people speak.
- Connection: Faulkner's narrative voices are vernacular. That marks class, geography, history — all at once.

**OBLIQUE**
- Origin: Latin *obliquus* (slanting, not straight, lateral)
- Weight: The saying of something by saying something else. The iceberg.
- Avoid instead: *indirect, unclear*
- Exercise: *"He did not look at her."* What does that sentence say? Nothing directly. Everything indirectly.

**IMPASSIVE**
- Origin: Latin *im-* (not) + *passivus* (capable of feeling, from *pati* = to suffer)
- Weight: Not emotionless — *ungoverned* by emotion. The face that gives nothing away.
- Avoid instead: *expressionless, cold*
- Connection: Hemingway's narrators are impassive. They see. They report. They do not explain what they feel.
