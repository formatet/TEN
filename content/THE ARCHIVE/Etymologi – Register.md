Register is not style — it is power. Which register you choose reveals who you are, who you are speaking to, and what you assume about the relationship between you.

The English Nobel teaches students to switch register deliberately. Not randomly.

---

## The three-year registers

| Year | Thread | Dominant registers | Typical words |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 – The Concrete | Objective observation | Physical, sensory, Anglo-Saxon | *earth, bone, blood, stone, sky* |
| Year 2 – The Social | Power and argument | Rhetorical, Latin-French, formal | *liberty, rhetoric, colonial, testimony* |
| Year 3 – The Existential | Consciousness and memory | Abstract, philosophical, fragmented | *consciousness, complicity, synthesis, reticence* |

The progression is deliberate. Students write their way upward in abstraction — but always carry Year 1's grounded, physical gaze.

---

## Register pairs to practise with

These pairs illustrate how register works in practice. Give students the pair — ask them to decide: who says this, and why?

| Low register | High register | The tension |
|---|---|---|
| *ask* | *enquire / interrogate* | Conversation vs interrogation |
| *begin* | *commence* | Meeting vs ceremony |
| *show* | *demonstrate / manifest* | Evidence vs display |
| *wrong* | *unjust / immoral* | Gut feeling vs argument |
| *die* | *pass away / expire* | Grief vs distance |

## Register in the Nobel texts

**Yeats** uses high register but makes it physical — *"the falcon cannot hear the falconer."* High register with Anglo-Saxon precision. The register says: this is serious, but the body is present.

**Churchill** is deliberately formal — *"We shall fight on the beaches"* — but with Anglo-Saxon simplicity at the core. The formality lifts it; the simplicity makes it credible.

**Morrison** breaks register to mark power. Slavery has a Latinate-formal register in legal documents; her characters live in the Anglo-Saxon body. When she moves between the two, the reader feels the violence of the shift.

---

## The examination tool

In pastiche tasks: ask students not just to *imitate* a Nobel style but to *explain* a register decision.

*"I chose 'incorruptible' rather than 'honest' because Coetzee's narrator would not use a simple Anglo-Saxon word for a quality he is uncertain about."*

That is the difference between producing and understanding.
