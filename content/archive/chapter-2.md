## The Music of the Image: Yeats, Tagore, Glück

*Part of [The Etymological Archive](/archive/) | Chapter thread: mechanics, not feeling. The line break is a tool.*

---

The words in this chapter have resonance. They describe time, light, voice, threshold. Poetry is not "expression" — it is precision about things that are difficult to see directly.

---

## The Words

**AUSTERE**
- Origin: Latin *austerus* (harsh, dry, severe) via Greek
- Weight: Free from ornament. Not cold — purified.
- Avoid instead: *simple, boring, cold*
- Why it matters: Glück is not simple — she is *austere*. That is craft, not lack.

**ELEGY**
- Origin: Greek *elegeia* (a lament, a song of mourning)
- Weight: A form with grief built into its structure — not a subject but a way of seeing
- Connection: Glück's *The Wild Iris* is elegiac without lamenting. Heaney's *North* is elegiac over a lost landscape.

**LYRIC**
- Origin: Greek *lyrikos* (sung to the lyre)
- Weight: Subjective, musical, directed at a listener. Distinct from epic (narrative) and dramatic (action).
- Exercise: What are you reading — lyric, epic or dramatic? The answer changes what you look for.

**THRESHOLD**
- Origin: Old English *therscold* (the sill one threshes on — the boundary between inside and out)
- Weight: Not a place but a condition. To stand on the threshold is not yet to have chosen.
- Connection: Glück's *The Wild Iris* opens with the flower stepping out of death. That is a threshold moment.

**EPHEMERAL**
- Origin: Greek *ephemeros* (from *epi* + *hemera* — lasting only a day)
- Weight: Not merely short-lived — designed to disappear. A dewdrop is ephemeral in a way a mountain is not.
- Connection: Tagore on the fleeting moment of insight. Yeats on the fleeting heroism of politics in *Easter, 1916*.

**LUMINOUS**
- Origin: Latin *lumen* (light)
- Weight: Not merely bright — illuminating, lit through. Giving light to what surrounds it.
- Avoid instead: *bright, clear*
