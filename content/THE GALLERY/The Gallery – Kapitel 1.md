## Pearl S. Buck (1892–1973), United States

Pearl Buck grew up in China. Her parents were missionaries. She learned Chinese before she learned English. She played in rice paddies and village streets. She watched farmers pull water from wells. She watched children eat and sleep and die.

When she wrote *The Good Earth*, she did not write about China from the outside. She wrote from inside a life she had lived. Wang Lung's hands are **coarse** from fieldwork. His wife O-Lan is **substantial** — heavy with labour, with silence, with what she carries and does not say.

Buck understood that the poor are not **peripheral**. They are the centre. The earth they work is the **relentless** fact that everything else grows from.

She received the Nobel Prize in 1938. She was the first American woman to win it.

## John Steinbeck (1902–1968), United States

John Steinbeck grew up in the Salinas Valley of California. He picked fruit as a young man. He knew what it felt like to be hungry and tired in a country that called itself rich.

*The Grapes of Wrath* follows the Joad family west. They drive a broken truck. The road is long and hot. The land they left is dust. The land they are going to does not want them.

Steinbeck's sentences are **stark**. They do not ask for your pity. They show you the turtle crossing the road and let you watch. They show you the dog killed by a car and move on. The world in this book has weight. It does not stop for grief.

He received the Nobel Prize in 1962. He said: *"A writer who does not passionately believe in the perfectibility of man has no dedication nor any membership in literature."*
