Rudyard Kipling was born in Bombay and sent to England as a boy. He came back to India as a young man and wrote about it with a clear, **detached** eye — the eye of someone who loved the place and also served the empire that ruled it.

In *Rikki-Tikki-Tavi* he watches a mongoose the way a naturalist watches: the pink nose, the restless tail, the war-cry in the grass. He notices everything. He does not soften the animal. He shows it.

But look to the **horizon** of this world and it is an imperial one. An English family in a bungalow. An Indian garden, half-wild and **arid** in the heat, full of danger. A small loyal creature who keeps the danger out. Kipling saw India exactly — and he saw it from above.

He won the Nobel Prize in 1907, the first writer in English to do so.
