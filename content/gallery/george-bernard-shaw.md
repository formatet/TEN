title: "George Bernard Shaw (1856–1950)"
publish: "true"
---
George Bernard Shaw understood that the English class system was maintained not by law or force but by accent. If you spoke as the upper classes spoke, you were assumed to be one of them. If you did not, no amount of intelligence or talent would compensate. *Pygmalion* — his most famous play — is a proof of this argument performed on stage.

His dialogue is **incorruptible** in its sharpness: every character speaks exactly as their position in the world requires, and Shaw shows you the machinery behind the performance. His wit is not ornamental. It is surgical. He uses laughter to keep the audience watching something they would otherwise find too uncomfortable to see.

He received the Nobel Prize in 1925. He initially refused it, calling prizes "a leper's bell" used to signal that a writer had stopped being dangerous. Then he accepted the money and donated it to fund translations of Swedish literature into English.
