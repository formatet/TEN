Three years. Nine chapters. Thirty-three writers who changed the language.

---

The English Nobel is a course in English built around the Nobel Prize in Literature — not because the prize is sacred, but because it is a constraint, a provocation, and a way to organise three years of serious work around something worth arguing about.

The laureates were chosen because they did something with English that no one had done before. The aim of the course is not that you know more about them. The aim is that after three years with their texts, you have more language with which to think.

The work is dictation, close reading, etymology, and imitation. The method is ancient. The texts are real.

---

## Three years

**Year One — The Eye.** The world as it actually is. The weight of it, the texture of it. Writers who looked with exact, patient attention before they interpreted anything.

*Kipling · Buck · Steinbeck · Yeats · Tagore · Glück · Hemingway · Faulkner · Lewis*

**Year Two — The Voice.** Argument, rhetoric, and historical position. Writers who used language as a weapon, a mirror, or a way of standing somewhere specific in the world.

*Churchill · Russell · Shaw · Dylan · Soyinka · Gordimer · Walcott · Gurnah · White · Golding · Munro · Bellow · Galsworthy · O'Neill*

**Year Three — The Mind.** Subtext, fragmentation, unreliable narrators, and the final synthesis. Writers who carried the weight of history into the form of the sentence itself.

*Lessing · Beckett · Pinter · Brodsky · Eliot · Morrison · Heaney · Coetzee · Naipaul · Ishiguro*
