This site is the course text for *The English Nobel*: an attempt at reading all English-language Nobel laureates in literature over three years, while remaining within the Swedish curriculum. It is written and run by [Timothy Kääriä](https://formatet.se), who teaches English and religion. The texts are written for the students who read them in class. Anyone else is welcome to read along.

---

## How it is made

This is not a published textbook. It is an open project, and it changes as the classes use it. The texts may be reused under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

The code and the website are produced by Claude Code, an AI tool made by Anthropic. Research and decision-making are also supported by large language models. The didactic decisions are made with the support of Wolfgang Klafki's five questions of didactic analysis, the progymnasmata (the graded writing exercises of classical rhetoric), and the Swedish military's teaching motto *visa, instruera, öva, öva, pröva*: show, instruct, practise, practise, test. The mistakes are mine. If you find one, tell me.

## What the site records

There are no accounts, no log-ins and no cookies, and nothing a student writes is ever sent to the site.

Visits are counted with [Umami](https://umami.is), which runs on the same server as the site, in Sweden. It records which pages are read, how far down, for how long, which links are followed, and which words are searched for or looked up. It also records the browser, the type of device and a rough location (country and city). It does not store IP addresses, and it cannot tell one reader from another. I can see that a page was read. I cannot see who read it.

The numbers are used for one thing: to see which parts of the course are actually read, so that the parts that are not can be rewritten. They are not shared with anyone and not sold.

When you select a word and look it up, you leave this site for [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org). The same goes for the links to NobelPrize.org, Project Gutenberg and the Internet Archive. Those sites have their own rules.

## The texts

Rudyard Kipling died in 1936, so his work is free to publish, and *Rikki-Tikki-Tavi* and *If—* are on this site in full. Pearl S. Buck and John Steinbeck are still in copyright. Their stories are handed out on paper in class under the copying licence that Swedish schools hold through [Bonus Copyright Access](https://www.bonuscopyright.se/), and they are never published here. In short, the licence lets a teacher copy 15 percent of a book, but no more than 15 pages, per student and half-year. The law behind it is [§ 13 of the Swedish Copyright Act](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1960729-om-upphovsratt-till-litterara-och_sfs-1960-729/#K2P13): teachers and students may use works to illustrate teaching, and where a licence like this one is available, the licence sets the terms. Quotations from other writers are short and say whose they are.
