A three-year map of the course. Two lessons per week, eighty minutes each. Every lesson begins with dictation. Every second lesson begins with five minutes of writing.

---

## Year One — The Eye · 2026/27

*The grammar of observation. Concrete description before interpretation.*

---

### Captain Cook and the English Language — Introduction (3 weeks, 6 lessons)

**Week 34 · 17 Aug**
- L1 — *Arrival.* Register-hunting: what language sounds like in different contexts. Pair work, track selection, source criticism.
- L2 — Source criticism: review and discussion. Research in pairs. One-sentence thesis.

**Week 35 · 24 Aug**
- L1 — *Naming the Land.* The five-paragraph essay: modelled, then begun.
- L2 — Presentation preparation. General review.

**Week 36 · 31 Aug**
- L1 — *The People of the Shore.* Presentations part one (3–4 pairs).
- L2 — Presentations part two. Bridge to Kipling: what happens when an outsider names a place?

---

### Chapter 1 — The Foundational Narrative (5 weeks, 10 lessons)

*Kipling · Buck · Steinbeck*

**Week 37 · 7 Sep** — Opening
- L1 — Chapter introduction: **A Note on This Chapter** + **Before We Begin: Three Objects.** How to read with the eye. Kipling biography (*The Gallery, Chapter 1*).
- L2 — **Part One – Kipling 1:** Observation, empire, resistance and love. *If—* as dictation candidate. *Kim*, Chapter 1 as anchor text.

**Week 38 · 14 Sep** — Kipling into Buck
- L1 — **Part One – Kipling 2:** Rhythm, gaze, the music of the sentence. Archive: *relentless, detached*.
- L2 — **Part Two – Buck 1:** Earth, body, labour, silence as dignity. *The Good Earth* opening chapters.

**Week 39 · 21 Sep** — Buck into Steinbeck
- L1 — **Part Two – Buck 2:** Concrete detail without sentiment. Archive: *substantial, arid, horizon*.
- L2 — **Part Three – Steinbeck 1:** Poverty, road, physical world. *The Grapes of Wrath*, opening chapters.

**Week 40 · 28 Sep** — Steinbeck and synthesis
- L1 — **Part Three – Steinbeck 2:** Description without feeling-words. Comparison Kipling / Buck / Steinbeck: what does each *look at*?
- L2 — Etymology lesson Chapter 1: all six words. Production sequence introduced.

**Week 41 · 5 Oct** — Writing and assessment
- L1 — Imitation workshop: **The Writing Task – The Exact Sentence.** Physical description without adjectives of emotion.
- L2 — Assessment / oral explanation / retrieval Chapter 1. **The Big Question** discussed.

---

### Chapter 2 — The Music of the Image (4 weeks, 8 lessons)

*Yeats · Tagore · Glück (+ Eliot, introduced)*

**Week 42 · 12 Oct** — Opening
- L1 — Chapter introduction: **A Note on This Chapter** + **Before We Begin.** From observation to image. Yeats biography (*The Gallery, Chapter 2*).
- L2 — **Part One – Yeats 1:** Image and sound. *The Second Coming*, *Easter 1916* as texts.

**Week 43 · 19 Oct** — Yeats into Tagore
- L1 — **Part One – Yeats 2:** Image structure and pressure. Retrieval: *detached*.
- L2 — **Part Two – Tagore 1:** Translation as language problem — what disappears? *Gitanjali* excerpts.

*Autumn break, week 44.*

**Week 45 · 2 Nov** — Glück and Eliot
- L1 — **Part Three – Glück 1:** Garden, mortality, precision. Archive: *lyric, image, symbol*.
- L2 — **Eliot introduction** (from Chapter 2): the limit of Year One. *Prufrock* as horizon — a text you are not yet meant to fully understand.

**Week 46 · 9 Nov** — Etymology and writing
- L1 — Etymology lesson Chapter 2: *concrete, economy, elegiac, metaphor*.
- L2 — Writing workshop: a poem without adjectives of feeling.

**Week 47 · 16 Nov** — Assessment
- L1 — Oral mini-presentation / peer review of poems.
- L2 — Assessment / repair / retrieval Chapters 1–2. **The Big Question** discussed.

### Chapter 3 — The American Document (5 weeks, 10 lessons)

*Hemingway · Faulkner · Lewis*

**Week 48 · 23 Nov** — Opening
- L1 — Chapter introduction: **A Note on This Chapter** + **Before We Begin.** What happens when you take everything away? Retrieval Chapters 1–2.
- L2 — **Part One – Hemingway 1:** The iceberg theory. Surface and omission. *Hills Like White Elephants* — what is the story about?

**Week 49 · 30 Nov** — Hemingway and Lewis
- L1 — **Part One – Hemingway 2:** Dialogue and the unsaid. *Hills Like White Elephants* close reading.
- L2 — **Part Three – Lewis 1:** Vernacular and social satire. *Main Street / Babbitt* excerpts.

**Week 50 · 7 Dec** — Faulkner
- L1 — **Part Two – Faulkner 1:** Syntax, time and the mind under pressure. *The Sound and the Fury* excerpts.
- L2 — Hemingway vs Faulkner: the sentence as a worldview. What does syntactic choice reveal?

**Week 51 · 14 Dec** — Etymology and writing
- L1 — Etymology lesson Chapter 3: *terse, laconic, stark, vernacular, oblique, impassive*.
- L2 — Imitation workshop: omission / vernacular / long sentence. **Before We Begin** returned to.

*Christmas break.*

**Week 1 (VT) · 7 Jan** — Writing and assessment
- L1 — **The Writing Task:** One event in two or three styles.
- L2 — Assessment / oral explanation / retrieval Chapters 1–3. **The Big Question** discussed.

### Spring Term — Buffer and Consolidation (weeks 2–22)

The three chapters are complete. The spring term is used for consolidation, national exam methodology (*NP*), extended writing, portfolio work, and retrieval across Chapters 1–3.

*No new Nobel content is introduced in Year One spring term.*

## Year Two — The Voice · 2027/28

*Argument, rhetoric, and historical position.*

---

### Chapter 4 — The Rhetoric of Power (5 weeks, 10 lessons)

*Churchill · Russell · Shaw · Dylan*

- L2 — **Part One – Churchill:** Repetition and national courage. *We Shall Fight on the Beaches* close reading.

**Week 35 · 30 Aug** — Russell and Shaw
- L1 — **Part Two – Russell:** Clarity as moral obligation. Nobel lecture.
- L2 — **Part Three – Shaw:** Accent, class, dialogue. *Pygmalion* as register drama.

**Week 36 · 6 Sep** — Dylan and etymology
- L1 — **Part Four – Dylan:** The question as argument. *Blowin' in the Wind* / Nobel lecture.
- L2 — Etymology lesson Chapter 4: *rhetoric, inevitable, deliberate*.

**Week 37 · 13 Sep** — Argument workshop
- L1 — **The Writing Task:** Argument workshop — thesis, support, counter-argument.
- L2 — Rhetorical analysis: choose a speech, identify technique.

**Week 38 · 20 Sep** — Writing and assessment
- L1 — Extended writing: argument built from rhetorical technique.
- L2 — Assessment / oral rhetorical analysis / retrieval Chapters 1–4. **The Big Question** discussed.

### Spring Term — Final Synthesis (weeks 2–15)

**Week 2 · 7 Jan** — The final exercise
- L1 — *Three years in one word.* Each student chooses one archive word and writes 150–200 words on what it means to them now.
- L2 — Return to Buck and Steinbeck: is the eye warmer or clearer?

**Weeks 3–6** — Final essay, oral synthesis, portfolio.

**Week 15 · 7 Apr** — Course examination (*kursprov*): reading comprehension, listening, language use.

---

*The course runs 127 lessons across three years. The archive holds approximately one hundred words. The nine chapters hold thirty-three writers. What holds them together is the question the course began with: how do you look at something exactly?*
